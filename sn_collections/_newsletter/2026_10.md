## News

### Apply now to serve as an applied research computing graduate student internship mentor in 2027! 

We are accepting applications to serve as an ARS applied research computing graduate student internship mentor in 2027! These internships allow graduate students at our partner universities who have strong data science and computational skills to spend either a summer or a semester working full time with an ARS mentor (or mentors) on an ARS research project. Please note that ARS mentors are not required to have specific data science or computing expertise! Rather, their role is to guide the scientific direction of the project and to contribute to the intern's professional development. 

Each 10-week internship includes a competitive stipend and travel funding for the participant to spend time onsite with their ARS mentor(s). We are working with three partner universities to recruit student participants. We want each of our interns to have an outstanding experience, which means we need outstanding ARS mentors and research projects! 

If you are interested in participating, please visit the [mentor application page](/opportunities/internship-mentors) for more information about how to prepare and submit your application. 

Applications are due by COB on Friday, December 4, 2026.

### Ceres maintenance update: new QOS definitions 

SCINet's Ceres supercomputer underwent routine maintenance on October 12 that included a variety of upgrades and improvements. Most of these changes are not directly user-facing, but one change you might notice is that the "Quality of Service" (QOS) definitions have been updated to better reflect the partitions now available on Ceres and how they are used. (See the [updates in our July newsletter](/news/newsletter/2026-07#ceres-partition-changes) for more information about recent partition changes on Ceres.) In Slurm, [a QOS](https://slurm.schedmd.com/qos.html) defines the resources available to a job and how the scheduler should handle the job. 

Previously, Ceres had a multitude of QOS definitions (e.g., "normal", "400thread", "memlimit", "large", "long", and more). This has now been simplified to the following: 

* "ceres": The default QOS for all jobs submitted to the "ceres" partition. 

* "scavenger": The default QOS for all jobs submitted to the "scavenger" partition. 

* "debug": A high-priority QOS with a maximum job time of 30 minutes, used for job debugging and troubleshooting. 

* "sandbox": The only QOS available to SCINet users without a SCINet project. 

 

Most of the time, you do not have to think about QOS definitions at all: you can simply submit jobs to your desired partition (usually "ceres") and Slurm will automatically apply the appropriate QOS. The one important exception to this is if you want to submit a job using the "debug" QOS, which can be useful for quickly troubleshooting job submissions. The "debug" QOS has a short maximum job time of 30 minutes but high priority, which means jobs will usually start quickly. For more information about using the "debug" QOS, see the user tip below! 

Technically, all of the old QOS definitions on Ceres are still available (although they are hidden in Open OnDemand), but they will be removed during the next Ceres maintenance, scheduled for February. If you have job scripts that use the old QOS definitions, please update them to use the new, simplified QOS definitions outlined above. 

 

### Atlas maintenance update 

SCINet's Atlas supercomputer underwent routine maintenance on October 20. This maintenance event did not result in any major user-facing changes. The only change you might notice is that the "ood" "Quality of Service" (QOS) definition no longer appears as an option for Open OnDemand interactive apps. This QOS definition was still available for historic reasons and is no longer needed. (See the Ceres update, above, and the user tip, below, for more information about QOS definitions on SCINet's supercomputers.) A big part of this maintenance event was preparing the core Atlas infrastructure for the installation of new compute and GPU nodes this fiscal year. 

 

### Enhancing location access to SCINet infrastructure: FY2026 update 

Through the SCINet-X project, SCINet has established dedicated, high-speed connections to SCINet computing and data storage systems for nearly all ARS locations. These connections facilitate efficient transfers of large datasets, but they require using a local "café machine" for access to the high-speed connection. We know from conversations with many SCINet users that this can be inconvenient, and we have developed a more flexible solution that supports expanding high-speed access to any local computing device that needs it. Our long-term goal is to make the café machines obsolete by offering SCINet users high-speed data transfer capabilities directly from their local workstation or laptop. We are pleased to report significant progress on this effort in FY2026, during which we were able to expand SCINet access (and eliminate the need for café machines) at nineteen ARS locations. We are excited to continue pushing this effort forward in FY2027! Please [contact us](mailto:scinet_vrsc@usda.gov) if you have questions or would like to learn more about the plans for your research location.

### SCINet User Tip
**Using the "debug" QOS on Ceres and Atlas**
{:.subheader}

When a supercomputer is busy or the resources you need are in high demand, a batch job might have to wait in the queue for quite some time (hours, e.g.) before it runs. It can be frustrating if, when the job finally does run, it immediately fails due to a bug in your sbatch script or elsewhere in your code. To avoid this situation, it is very helpful to be able to quickly launch a job to verify that it works as expected or, if not, iterate on fixing any problems. 

This is exactly what the "debug" Quality of Service (QOS) definitions on Ceres and Atlas are for! Among other things, a [Slurm QOS]( https://slurm.schedmd.com/qos.html) can define the priority a job has for scheduling as well as its maximum running time. On both Ceres and Atlas, submitting a job with the "debug" QOS gives the job a higher priority than usual, but it also limits the maximum running time to 30 minutes. This combination is often perfect for debugging: the higher priority and short running time mean that Slurm will start the job very quickly, which is ideal for iterative testing when developing sbatch scripts for long-running jobs. Once the script is ready, it can be submitted without the "debug" QOS and will then be queued as usual, without elevated priority or short running-time restrictions. 

To submit a job using the "debug" QOS, use sbatch's `--qos` (or `-q`) option. For example, suppose you have an sbatch script called "important_job.sh". To submit your job using the "debug" QOS, you could run: 

``` 
sbatch --qos=debug important_job.sh 
```  

Remember that the maximum job running time for the "debug" QOS is 30 minutes, so if your sbatch script specifies a longer running time (which will usually be the case!), the command above will fail. In that case, you can override the running time in the script by setting a shorter maximum running time from the command line. For example: 

``` 
sbatch --qos=debug --time=00:10:00 important_job.sh 
```  

That way, you can submit your job with a shorter time request without modifying your sbatch script.
